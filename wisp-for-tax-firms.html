A Written Information Security Plan your PTIN renewal can stand behind.

When you renew your PTIN, Form W-12 asks you to confirm, under penalties of perjury, that your firm has a written data security plan. The FTC Safeguards Rule requires one, and IRS Publication 4557 sets out what it should cover. Clyvara writes yours with you — tailored to how your firm actually works, not a template with your name pasted in. One fixed fee: $1,250. Delivered within 10 business days.

PTINs expire December 31, 2026, and the renewal window is open now. For solo preparers, enrolled agents, CPAs, and small accounting firms.
What You Get

A plan that describes your firm, written to the rule.

The Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314) treats a tax preparation practice as a financial institution and requires a written information security program. This service produces the program, and the understanding that has to go with it:

One
The WISP document itself. Your firm’s written information security plan, covering the Safeguards Rule elements as they apply at your size: a designated qualified individual, access controls, encryption, multifactor authentication, staff training, vendor oversight, and an incident response plan. Where the Rule gives smaller firms a simpler path, the plan takes it and says so.
Two
A crosswalk to the sources. Each section of the plan is tied to the Safeguards Rule element and the IRS guidance (Publications 4557 and 5708) it answers, so an IRS or FTC question about any line has a one-minute answer.
Three
A staff walkthrough. Thirty minutes together over the finished plan, so the people named in it know what it asks of them. A plan nobody has read does not survive its first incident, or its first audit.
Four
An honest gap list. Anything the plan commits to that is not yet true in your firm, named plainly, with the fix and a sensible order for making it true. The renewal confirmation asks whether a plan exists; this is how the plan becomes real as well as written.
Five
An annual review date. The Rule expects the program to be maintained, not filed once and forgotten. Your first annual review is scheduled at delivery, before next year’s renewal season.
How It Works

Four steps, ten business days.

1Fit call

Thirty minutes on your firm: who prepares, what systems hold taxpayer data, which vendors touch it. If you already hold a plan that meets the Rule, you hear that here and no fee is taken.

2Information pass

A short, structured questionnaire about your practice, your software, and how returns and client files actually move through the firm. Most firms complete it in under an hour.

3Drafting

Clyvara drafts the plan against the Safeguards Rule and the IRS sample structure in Publication 5708, written around your answers — your tools, your people, your vendors, named as they are.

4Delivery and walkthrough

The plan, the crosswalk, and the gap list, delivered within 10 business days of the fit call, followed by the staff walkthrough and your scheduled annual review.

What it is
A tailored Written Information Security Plan for one tax or accounting firm, with the crosswalk, gap list, walkthrough, and annual review date. Led and delivered personally by Clyvara’s principal consultant.
What it is not
Not legal advice, and not a template download. Not IT implementation — Clyvara is a governance advisory, and where the gap list calls for tools or configuration, your IT provider does that work. Not a promise about any regulator’s decision; it is a plan written to the published rule and guidance.
Who it fits
Paid preparers renewing a PTIN, and the small accounting firms around them, who checked the box last year on a plan that was thin, borrowed, or never written. Also firms whose cyber insurer or largest client has started asking the same questions.

Clyvara LLC is a cybersecurity governance advisory. This service is consulting on a written security program; it is not legal, tax, or accounting advice, and it does not replace your own professional judgment about your firm’s obligations.
