What You Get

A rehearsal built around your practice, and the truth in writing.

The HIPAA Security Rule expects a practice to have security incident procedures, and cyber insurers increasingly ask when the team last exercised them. A plan in a drawer answers neither. This service tests the plan, and the people, while the stakes are still imaginary:

One
A scenario fitted to your practice. Written from a short intake on your systems, sites, and vendors: your practice management or EHR platform goes down, a ransom demand arrives, patients and referral partners start calling. The details are yours, so the decisions are real.
Two
The facilitated session. Half a day with your leadership, front desk, and billing leads in the room. New facts land as the morning unfolds, the way they would in life, and your team works each one: who calls the insurer, who talks to patients, what can be said, what gets shut down, in what order.
Three
A written findings note. Delivered within 5 business days: what held, what broke, where decisions stalled, and the first three fixes in priority order. It reads the way an insurer or an auditor would want to read it, because it is written for them as much as for you.
Four
An honest read on your incident plan. If the exercise shows your written procedures would carry the day, you hear that. If there is no written plan, or the plan names people who have left, that is finding number one, stated plainly, with the route to fixing it.
Five
A debrief that ends in decisions. Thirty minutes together over the findings note, so the fixes get owners and dates before the exercise becomes a memory. Readiness that is not scheduled is not readiness.
How It Works

Four steps, about two weeks end to end.

1Fit call

Thirty minutes on your practice: size, sites, systems, and when your team last rehearsed anything like this. If you already exercise regularly under a current plan, you hear that here and no fee is taken.

2Intake and scenario build

A short, structured intake on how the practice actually runs, then Clyvara writes the scenario around it: your platform, your locations, your vendors, your busiest morning. Participants get a one-page brief beforehand, nothing more; the surprises stay surprises.

3The session

Half a day, facilitated by Clyvara’s principal consultant. Your team decides in real time as the scenario escalates; every decision, delay, and disagreement is noted, because those are the findings.

4Findings and debrief

The written findings note within 5 business days of the session, followed by the debrief call: fixes in priority order, owners named, dates set.

What it is
A facilitated ransomware tabletop exercise for one healthcare practice, with a tailored scenario, the findings note, and the debrief. Led and delivered personally by Clyvara’s principal consultant, under the SHIELD Incident Readiness service.
What it is not
Not a penetration test, not a technical audit, and not a HIPAA compliance certification. Clyvara is a governance advisory: the exercise tests decisions, roles, and procedures. Where findings call for technical work, your IT provider does that work.
Who it fits
Practices whose insurer, lender, or largest referral partner has started asking how an incident would be handled, and practices that have a plan on paper that nobody has ever run. Multi-location groups get the most from it, because the second site is where coordination breaks first.
