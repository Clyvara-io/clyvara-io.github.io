The Questions

Fifteen questions a buyer’s team will ask.

A good answer is evidence, not adjectives: a document, a setting, a date, a name. If several of these would take you more than a week to answer today, that is the gap this review closes.

Data and access

  1. What personal or customer data does the business hold, and where does it live? (A named inventory, not “on the computers.”)
  2. Who has administrator access to your key systems, and when was that list last reviewed?
  3. Is multi-factor authentication on for email, banking, and remote access, for everyone?
  4. When an employee leaves, who removes their access, and how fast?

Systems and vendors

  1. Which systems would stop the business if they went down for a week?
  2. When were your backups last proven by an actual restore?
  3. Which outside providers can reach your data (IT support, payroll, bookkeeping, industry platforms), and what have they signed?
  4. Is everything still supported and patched, including the machine nobody touches?

Incidents and policies

  1. Has the business ever had a cyber incident, a fraudulent payment, or a compromised mailbox? How was it handled? (Buyers forgive a handled incident. They punish a discovered one.)
  2. Do you have written information security policies, and when were they last updated?
  3. If ransomware encrypted your systems tonight, what are the first three things that happen tomorrow morning?
  4. Do any of your customer contracts promise anything about security or breach notification?

Insurance and evidence

  1. Do you carry cyber insurance, what does it cover, and was the application answered accurately?
  2. Could you produce, within one week, a list of your systems, your policies, and your key vendors?
  3. Who in the business can answer a buyer’s cyber questions on a call, with documents to back the answers?
