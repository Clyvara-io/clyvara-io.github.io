The Security Questionnaire Readiness Checklist

The evidence customers, insurers, and funders actually ask for — and how to have it ready before they ask.

Sooner or later, someone your business depends on will ask how you protect the information they trust you with — a customer doing vendor due diligence, a cyber insurer at renewal, a funder reviewing a grant. The hard part is rarely the questions — it is the evidence: documents, records, and screenshots that prove what you say is true. Tick every item you could produce within one business day. The unticked boxes are your real exposure, and your work plan.

1Know what you have

  • An inventory of computers, servers, and devices your business uses
  • A list of cloud services and software, and who administers each one
  • A data map: what customer, patient, or client data you hold, and where it lives
  • A simple diagram or written summary of how your network fits together

2Control who gets in

  • Multi-factor authentication enforced on email, remote access, and admin accounts
  • A named account for every user — no shared logins
  • A current list of accounts with administrator rights
  • A written joiner/leaver process for granting and removing access
  • A dated record of your most recent access review

3Keep systems defended

  • A patching schedule, and the most recent patch or update report
  • Endpoint protection on every device, with a coverage list to prove it
  • A recent vulnerability scan or equivalent check, with results
  • Email filtering and anti-phishing protection in place

4Back up — and prove recovery

  • A documented backup schedule: what is backed up, how often, and where
  • Backups protected against ransomware (offline, immutable, or access-controlled)
  • A dated record of a restore test — backups you have actually recovered from
  • A stated retention period that matches your policy

5Be ready for an incident

  • A written incident response plan, however short
  • A contact list for the first phone calls: IT provider, insurer, bank, legal counsel
  • Notes from a tabletop exercise, a real incident, or lessons learned
  • Your cyber insurance policy details page, if you carry cover

6Govern it on paper

  • An information security policy that is dated and signed
  • Your most recent risk assessment
  • A risk register: each significant risk, its owner, and its treatment
  • A remediation tracker showing findings you have closed, not just opened

7Prepare your people

  • Security awareness training records for staff
  • Phishing-awareness activity or results, if you run them
  • Signed acceptable-use acknowledgments
  • Background checks where a role or a contract requires them

8Manage your vendors

  • An inventory of vendors that access your systems or your data
  • Signed data-protection or business-associate agreements where they apply
  • A security review or questionnaire response on file for critical vendors
  • Breach-notification commitments in your vendor contracts

If several boxes are unticked, you are not unusual — you are unready.
