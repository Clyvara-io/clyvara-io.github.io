What's Inside

Eight areas. Thirty-five pieces of evidence.

Every item on the checklist is phrased as evidence — something you could produce within one business day if a customer, insurer, or funder asked for it tomorrow.

1Know what you have

Your device and software inventory, who administers each system, and a map of where customer or patient data actually lives.

2Control who gets in

MFA, named accounts with no shared logins, your admin list, and proof that leavers lose access the day they leave.

3Keep systems defended

Patching records, endpoint protection coverage, and recent vulnerability results you can hand over, not describe.

4Back up — and prove recovery

A backup schedule is a claim. A dated restore test is evidence. Questionnaires increasingly ask for the second one.

5Be ready for an incident

A written response plan, the first phone calls listed, and notes showing the plan has been walked through at least once.

6Govern it on paper

A signed security policy, a current risk assessment, a risk register with owners, and findings shown closed — not just opened.

7Prepare your people

Training records, phishing-awareness results, and signed acceptable-use acknowledgments for the whole team.

8Manage your vendors

Which vendors can touch your systems or data, the agreements you hold with them, and the reviews you have on file.

How To Use It

Ten honest minutes, once a quarter.

Print it or keep it on screen. Work down the list and tick only what you could actually produce within one business day — not what you believe is probably in place.

Step one
Tick what you could produce within one business day.
Step two
The unticked boxes are your real exposure — roughly in the order a questionnaire will find them.
Step three
Treat the unticked list as your work plan, and re-run the checklist each quarter to watch it shrink.
